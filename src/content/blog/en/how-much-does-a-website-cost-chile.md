If you're getting quotes for your first website, you've probably noticed something odd: offers can range from around CLP $100,000 to well over a million pesos for what looks, on paper, like the same thing. That's not because some providers are underpricing and others overcharging — it's because "a website" can mean very different projects depending on scope, and that's exactly what explains the price gap.

## Why prices vary so much

A website's price depends on how much real work is behind it: how much custom design it needs, how many pages and features it has, how solid the technical side is, and what's included after launch (hosting, support, maintenance). A simple template-based landing page isn't comparable to a corporate site with several sections, its own contact form and basic SEO, and that's not comparable to an online store with payments and shipping logistics either. Comparing quotes without looking at scope is comparing different things under the same label.

## Landing page, corporate site, or online store: not the same thing

A **simple landing page** works to present a business, list basic services, and route inquiries to WhatsApp or a simple form — a solid starting point. A **professional or corporate site** organizes several pages (home, services, about, contact), has its own contact form, and includes basic SEO so people can actually find you on Google. An **online store** adds a completely different layer: catalog, payment methods, shipping, order management — it's a different kind of project, not a "bigger version" of a corporate site, which is why it almost never has a fixed price: it depends on what the business needs to sell and how.

## What a serious quote should include

A clear quote should tell you, at minimum, how many pages it covers, what happens with content (do you write it, or do they help?), how many rounds of revisions are included before extra changes get billed separately, whether hosting and the domain are included and for how long, and what's explicitly out of scope. If a quote doesn't clarify these points, it's hard to know what you're actually paying for.

## Costs that can show up after launch

This is where most surprises happen, and it's worth being careful here. It helps to keep four things clearly separate:

The **domain** is the web address registered in your name (e.g., yourbusiness.cl); it has an annual renewal cost, usually modest, that exists regardless of who builds your site. **Hosting** is the infrastructure your site runs on; many providers include the first year and charge for renewal starting the second. **Maintenance** is a separate, optional service: someone handling monitoring, backups, updates, and small content changes so you don't have to. And **corporate email** (addresses like contact@yourbusiness.cl) is, technically, a different service from website hosting — not every site needs it, and when it is needed, it's usually quoted separately because it depends on what kind of email infrastructure is used.

On top of that, **special features** (online stores, bookings, payments, integrations with other systems) are almost never included in a base plan, precisely because each one involves real additional work.

Any serious provider should explain all of this upfront, not leave you to find out at the second-year renewal.

## Signs a quote isn't being clear

It's worth being cautious when a quote doesn't mention how many pages or revisions are included, when it promises "guaranteed top Google rankings" (no one can honestly guarantee that), when what happens to hosting after the first year is left vague, or when the price is very low but nobody explains what's excluded.

## Questions worth asking before hiring anyone

How many pages and revisions are included, exactly? Who writes the copy, and who uploads it? Are hosting and the domain included, and what happens in year two? Is maintenance mandatory or optional? What happens if I need a feature later that wasn't part of the original plan? A provider who answers these clearly, without dodging, is usually a good sign.
